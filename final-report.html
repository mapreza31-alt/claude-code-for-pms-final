Rook Dispatch · Final report · Claude Code for PMs

Release 4.2 didn't make responders stop answering.
It stopped asking them.

Six modules, one question: what went wrong after 12 August? This report shows the answer, the prompts that found it, the fix I prototyped, and the skill that catches the next one before it ships.

Maria Apreza, Dispatch PM · 7 Nov 2026 · Data: rook.pings, support tickets, September interviews, dispatch-routing code, rook-wiki · Use ↓ / ↑ or scroll

The bottom line

What happened, in plain words

  1. 4.2 cut the ping wait from 90 to 60 seconds.
  2. More pings ran out before anyone answered. Those count as missed.
  3. The routing code scores a miss exactly like a "no" (−0.12) and never lets the score recover.
  4. A few early misses pushed four responders down the order. With no pings, they couldn't earn their way back.
  5. Their work moved to three others. Unfilled callouts doubled. Handlers saw "my phone never goes off."

What it wasn't

Not the August dip. The week of 3 Aug was the best week on record (77.9% taken). Misses jumped on release day.

Not people saying no. Turn-downs actually fell, from 21% to 18%. The whole drop is misses.

Not a reason to revert 4.2. Wide-area responders asked for the ranking change. The fault is how a miss is scored.

Why four people disappeared

A loop with no way out

The only way to raise your score is to take a ping. A low score means you don't get pings. So once you fall, you stay down.

Source: config.py (DECLINE_PENALTY = 0.12, ACCEPTANCE_CREDIT = 0.08; 4.2 weights proximity 0.60, acceptance 0.25), history.py (no recovery over time, no reset). Since 19 Aug the four still miss 75% of pings even when first in line (others: 16%), so the cause of those misses is still open with Wen.

Handler sees

Where each responder sits in the order and why. "No answer in 60 s. Counted as a miss, not a no." A flag when someone goes quiet, before anyone files a ticket.

Responder sees

"Missing it doesn't count against you like a no." Then "You're back in rotation." A "Tap if you got this" check that doesn't touch their score.

4.3 underneath

Score a miss separately, with recovery over a few days. A score floor. An unfilled-callout alert. Reset the four, but only once we know why they miss.

Keeping it from happening again · Module 6

The review-checklist skill

4.2 didn't fail because someone wrote bad code. It failed because questions had no owner and nobody was measuring misses. Wen's 2019 note asking whether the score should ease back was never answered. Marcus's 14 Aug question was never answered. The skill checks every brief for exactly these gaps before it goes anywhere.

1
Names who owns it.
A named person, not "TBD", not someone who has left. 4.2: Wen's question had no owner.
2
Says how we'll know it worked.
A metric with a baseline and target. 4.2: nobody watched the miss rate after cutting the wait.
3
Keeps the same scope start to finish.
Flags items that creep in or quietly drop out.
4
Explains the problem before the fix.
